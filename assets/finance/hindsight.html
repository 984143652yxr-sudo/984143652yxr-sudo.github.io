This panel varies a common mean-return target q. The ES-budget slider in Figure 4 is a separate comparison.

Blue · trained ES allocations
wE(q) = arg min Htrainα(w), subject to mtrain(w) ≥ q.
Purple dashed · trained Markowitz allocations
wM(q) = arg min wᵀΣ̂trainw, subject to mtrain(w) ≥ q.
Green dotted · hindsight ES frontier
wO(u) = arg min Htestα(w), subject to mtest(w) ≥ u.
Fits and evaluates on the entire 2020–2025 period; an optimistic reference with look-ahead information.

All curves use the same plotted mean and historical ES. Upper-left is favorable. Each point is a fixed target-weight portfolio, maintained by daily rebalancing. ◆ is equal weight.

Blue and purple evaluation paths can bend backwards: training-efficient weights need not stay efficient later. Curves connect computed allocations; they do not represent wealth over time. Hindsight uses one constant target-weight vector per point, with no costs.

The hindsight gap is the potential ES reduction at at least the same realised mean, within this fixed-weight universe. It includes the advantage of knowing future returns and does not establish a deployable strategy. Markowitz uses the first two moments; its optimization is defined for any finite-covariance distribution.
