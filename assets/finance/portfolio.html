Three different comparisons: H − N is risk underestimation for identical weights; H − b is excess over the training budget; the later-sample ES difference compares two allocations. A percentage-point ES difference is a difference in tail averages, rather than an extra loss incurred every day.

Weights are fitted on 2010–2019 and held at fixed targets by daily rebalancing in 2020–2025. Costs are excluded. Later-period outcomes also reflect market changes; the comparison does not isolate a causal effect of the normal assumption.

Construction and reproducibility

Published universe: 10Ind in DeMiguel, Garlappi, Nogales & Uppal (2009), Table 1. This is a new daily-data mean–ES exercise using that universe. Its periods, objective and estimates differ from the paper's monthly experiment.

Each budget selects a precomputed optimum among all ten long-only weights. Historical optimization: linear programming. Normal optimization: convex mean–ES constraint. Curves connect solutions at 51 budget levels; intermediate curve segments are visual guides. Infeasible budgets show no optimal allocation.
